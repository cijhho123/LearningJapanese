Two linked academic papers that give the linguistic/computational backbone
under this guide's is-vs-ga and "invisible subject" discussion. Both study
zero pronouns (unexpressed verb arguments) in real Japanese text and how
wa/ga/o/ni marking plus "centering" (which entity a passage is about)
constrain what a dropped argument refers to. The 1994 paper proposes a
"zero topic assignment" rule tested against native-speaker judgements on
minimal discourse pairs; the 1996 chapter tests the resulting algorithm
against 250 utterances from 20 Japanese newspaper articles.

Not a teaching resource - this is the scholarly literature the guide's
"is Japanese really subjectless" section is implicitly drawing on. Public
via arXiv (author self-archival of published/in-press work); cite the
original venues above.
