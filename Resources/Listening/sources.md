# Sources and provenance - Listening

Everything added to this folder, where it came from, and what you are and are not allowed to do
with it.

The guiding constraint is **text over audio**. Audio is large, usually not redistributable, and
this folder has a hard size budget; transcripts and subtitle files are tiny, legally cleaner, and
are exactly what the guide's [active listening rep](<./readme.md#the-active-listening-rep>)
requires - you cannot "listen raw, then verify" without something to verify against.

### Subtitle selection - and why these shows

Matched to the guide's [rung 3](<./readme.md#rung-3---slice-of-life-anime-and-easy-drama-months-3-18>) argument
that **domain narrowness**, not popularity, is what makes a show learnable. Official Netflix
closed captions were preferred over fansubs where both existed, because they are accurate.

| Show | Files | Why it is in the set |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| Shirokuma Cafe (しろくまカフェ) | 24 | Routinely named the single easiest anime for learners. Short, slow, everyday cafe talk. |
| Non Non Biyori | 12 | Official CC. The canonical "slowest, gentlest, narrowest vocabulary" recommendation. |
| Yuru Camp (ゆるキャン△) | 12 | Narrow domain - camping, food, weather - and lots of single-speaker narration. |
| Karakai Jouzu no Takagi-san | 12 | Two-speaker school dialogue, near-continuous conversation, contemporary casual register. |
| Komi-san wa, Comyushou desu | 12 | Official Netflix CC. Deliberately slow, heavily paused delivery. |
| Skip and Loafer | 12 | Official Netflix CC. Contemporary teen conversation; includes Ishikawa dialect, useful for the guide's [dialect chapter](<./readme.md#dialects-as-a-listening-problem>). |
| Crayon Shin-chan (sample) | 3 | Sample only. A children's show whose speech is heavily contracted and reduced - direct practice material for the contractions chapter. |
| Sazae-san (sample) | 8 | Sample only. The longest-running family anime; plain, standard household register across three generations. |

All 95 files contain properly timestamped subtitles with substantial Japanese text (at least 100
characters each).

## Honest licence caveats

Read this before publishing this repository anywhere.

1. **NHK Easy Japanese is "personal use only."** NHK's download page says so explicitly. It is
   free of charge and this is a personal study archive, which is the sanctioned use - but it is
   **not** licensed for redistribution. If this repo is ever made public, remove
   `NHK World - Easy Japanese/`.
2. **The kitsunekko subtitles are a grey area.** kitsunekko is an unofficial archive; the files
   are a mix of fan-made subtitles and extracted official closed captions. No licence is stated
   or offered. The anime dialogue itself is copyrighted by its rights holders. Widely used by the
   learner community, legally unsettled, and **not** something to redistribute.
3. **Four items are genuinely redistributable: the two Wikipedia references (CC BY-SA 4.0), the
   Kansai-ben Primer (CC BY-SA 3.0), and Preadvanced Japanese (CC BY-NC 4.0).** Everything else in
   this folder is free-of-charge-for-personal-use or grey-area, not freely republishable. That is
   an honest summary of the state of Japanese listening material: the good stuff is free to *use*
   and almost none of it is free to *republish*.

**The important negative result:** **Nihongo con Teppei**, the podcast this repo's own media sheet
ranks "Very Easy" and calls "perfect for a first podcast", has **no transcript** - and neither do
the other Teppei series, Kevinvin sleepy Japanese, Learn Japanese from real talks, Nあ～ casual
nihongo, and きまぐれ日本語. The most-recommended beginner podcast is therefore the one you
*cannot* do a checkable active rep with. If you want the diagnosis step, use Let's Talk in Japanese
or Sakura Tips instead, and keep Teppei for volume.
