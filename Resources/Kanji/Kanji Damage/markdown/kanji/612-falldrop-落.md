堕落(だらく) してる |  corrupt ★★★☆☆   
[堕](1101-corrupt-堕.html) (corrupt) + [落](612-falldrop-落.html) (fall,drop) = 堕落 (corrupt)  corrupt in one's personal life - not usually political corruption. Drugs, lies, trife behavior.  
部落民(ぶらくみん) |  burakumin ★★☆☆☆ [CCW](../tags/4.html)[FR](../tags/9.html)[ABU](../tags/1.html)   
[部](795-section-部.html) (section) + [落](612-falldrop-落.html) (fall,drop) + [民](1165-folk-民.html) (folk) = 部落民 (burakumin)  (literally,'the fallen group.') this is - along with Ainu, Korean ex-slaves, the Eta-hin,and the handicapped, one of the main groups discriminated against in Japan. I won't go into the whole story here, but most Japanese will say, 'Don't say 部落民, it's 差別 (さべつ＝ discriminatory)' What they neglect to tell you is, there is no polite word! Effectively denying someone a voice in the guise of being polite to them is a very. Very. VERY Japanese way of fucking with people. However, you can say 同和 (どうわ = social integration) instead: speaking of the phenomenon instead of the individuals. For instance, discrimination against people is 同和の問題 ('the social integration problem'). A nice article explaining the importance of using the right language is [here](http://www.hikosaemon.com/2011/11/when-talking-about-burakumin-never-say.html) and a more general overview of discrimination is [here](http://www.tofugu.com/2011/11/18/the-burakumin-japans-invisible-race/).  
落第(らくだい) する |  fail a test ★★☆☆☆   
[落](612-falldrop-落.html) (fall,drop) + [第](894-rank-or-number-in-series-第.html) (rank or number in series) = 落第 (fail a test)  fail an exam,or get held back a grade.
