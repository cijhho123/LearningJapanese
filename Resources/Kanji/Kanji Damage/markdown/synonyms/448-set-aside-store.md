##  set aside, store

[蓄](../kanji/208-store-or-put-aside-蓄.html)える(たくわえる) |  Setting stuff aside for a rainy day. Used about good things. Also – in contrast to 貯める、 蓄えるis used about important things:bears accumulating winter fat for their hibernation. One’s retirement nest egg.   
---|---  
[蓄](../kanji/208-store-or-put-aside-蓄.html)[積](../kanji/553-pile-up-積.html)(ちくせき) |  Accumulate. Used about bad things mostly – bills or stress pile up. But it also can be good, like the compound interest or accumulated knowledge.   
[貯](../kanji/538-save-up-貯.html)める(ためる) |  Store up unimportant things; things that you didn’t deliberately seek out but accumulated nonetheless:change from your couch cushions. “I left the bathtub full for you if you want to use it.”   
[留](../kanji/1264-absent-stopped-留.html)まってきた(とまってきた) |  Half-done things pile up. Always bad. Bills, two weeks’ worth of half-done homework that is all due tomorrow, ohhhh shiiiiittt!!!   
[滞](../kanji/826-delay-be-overdue-滞.html)る(とどこおる) |  Unlike 留まってきた (weeks or months worth of stuff piling up)、 滞る is used about bad things that are piling up right now:traffic jams, delays. The idea of 滞る is that things which are currently in progress. . . .don’t progress.   
[重](../kanji/1059-heavy-overlap-重.html)なる(かさなる) |  Used when a series of bad things happen to you. It never rains but it pours. Yesterday my boyfriend dumped me, then I lost my cell phone, and now I just lost $200 playing pachinko.   
[積](../kanji/553-pile-up-積.html)[重](../kanji/1059-heavy-overlap-重.html)ねる(つみかさねる) |  A super-strong version of 重なる。 積み重ねる is like, you have such bad luck you’re suicidal.
