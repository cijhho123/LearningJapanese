Two sources on shadowing (シャドーイング), the technique this guide calls "the one procedure to
take away from this guide" - previously described here with a 7-step procedure and no citations
behind it at all. Both are freely downloadable; neither carries an explicit redistribution
licence - see sources.md.

---

Sakoda Kumiko (n.d.), "Shadoiingu no miryoku to nazo - Nihongo no shuutoku kara shadoiingu no
jissen made" [シャドーイングの魅力と謎 - 日本語の習得からシャドーイングの実践まで - "The Charm and
Mystery of Shadowing: From Japanese Acquisition to Shadowing Practice"], research-meeting slide
deck, Hiroshima University Graduate School of Education.
https://gsjal.jp/toda/dat/kenkyuukai1102.pdf (hosted on Waseda University's Graduate School of
Japanese Applied Linguistics site - Toda Takako's phonetics/pronunciation-teaching lab)
No separate licence statement found; credit Sakoda Kumiko / Hiroshima University.

Sakoda is a leading Japanese second-language-acquisition researcher (her name recurs throughout
the shadowing literature, including later co-authoring the 2019 book "Nihongo kyoushi no tame no
shadoiingu shidou" - Shadowing Instruction for Japanese Teachers). This is a 36-slide
conference/workshop handout, not a peer-reviewed paper, but it is the single most useful item
found here for the guide's shadowing section specifically because it is a synthesis: it connects
general second-language-acquisition theory to shadowing research, and surveys what the prior
shadowing literature has and has not established, rather than reporting one narrow experiment.
No exact date is stated in the file; the filename ("kenkyuukai1102") suggests a November 2nd
research-meeting talk.

Mochizuki Michiko (2006), "Shadoiinguhou no Nihongo kyouiku e no ouyou wo saguru - gakushuusha no
Nihongo nouryoku to shadoiingu no kouka ni taisuru gakushuusha hyouka to no kanrensei wo chuushin
ni -" [シャドーイング法の日本語教育への応用を探る―学習者の日本語能力とシャドーイングの効果に対する
学習者評価との関連性を中心に― - "Exploring the Application of the Shadowing Method to Japanese
Language Education"], Kansai University Audio-Visual Education (関西大学視聴覚教育), Vol.29,
pp.37-53 (31 March 2006).
https://www.kansai-u.ac.jp/fl/publication/pdf_aids/29/1-4_mochizuki.pdf
No separate licence statement found; official Kansai University publication server. Credit
Mochizuki Michiko / Kansai University.

A full-length article (17 pages) that situates shadowing within the wider family of interpreter-
training techniques - quick response, paraphrasing, summarisation, retention, sight translation,
consecutive and simultaneous interpretation, repeating, dictation, slash reading - and examines
which of these actually transfer to general Japanese-language ability, and how learner self-
evaluation of shadowing correlates with measured proficiency. Useful precisely because the guide
presents shadowing as a single fixed 7-step drill; this paper's frame (shadowing as one technique
in a family, borrowed from professional interpreter training) explains *why* the drill is built
the way it is. Text in this PDF has a CJK font-encoding quirk in this environment: English
loanwords (the technique names above) extract correctly with pdftotext, confirming genuine
academic content, even though pdftoppm cannot render the Japanese glyphs locally without an
Adobe-Japan1 CMap resource this machine does not have installed.
