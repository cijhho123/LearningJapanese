Four academic papers, gathered together because they are the empirical/scholarly backing this
guide's Pronunciation and Pitch Accent sections did not have. All four are freely downloadable
from their publisher (J-STAGE or the author's university), but none carries an explicit
redistribution licence - see the caveat at the end of each entry and in sources.md.

---

Matsuzaki Hiroshi, Kushida Machiko, Josei Hyakutaro, Tsukiji Nobumi, Liu Mingjie (1994),
"Puroso dii-hyo wo mochiita shizen na Nihongo shuutoku no tame no onsei kyouiku no kouka"
[プロソディー表を用いた自然な日本語習得のための音声教育の効果 - "The effect of phonetic
education using a 'Prosody Table' for natural Japanese acquisition"], Journal of Japanese
Language Teaching Methods (日本語教育方法研究会誌), Vol.1 No.3, pp.20-21.
https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jlem/1/3/1_KJ00008196379/_article/-char/ja/
© 1994 Japanese Language Teaching Methods Research Association.

Directly answers the guide's "should you study pitch accent deliberately" question with real
data: the authors built a "Prosody Table" - a visualisation of the actual pitch curve of natural
Tokyo speech, rather than just accent-nucleus numbers or intonation marks - and used it to teach
12 Korean learners. Pronunciation improved more than with the conventional accent-mark-only
method. Short (2 pages, a research-report format typical of this journal), but a genuine
controlled comparison, not an opinion piece. Note that the CJK text in this PDF does not extract
cleanly with pdftotext in every environment (a font-encoding quirk in the original scan); it was
verified by rendering it to an image and reading it directly - the content is real and legible.

Kanamura Kumi (2020), "Nihongo akusento shidou wo shien suru tame no onseigaku kyouiku to wa -
Nihongo kyouin e no ankeeto chousa kara -" [日本語アクセント指導を支援するための音声学教育とは
―日本語教員へのアンケート調査から― - "Improvements to Phonetic Education That Would Aid in
Teaching Japanese Pitch Accent: Suggestions from a Questionnaire Survey of Japanese Language
Teachers"], Journal of the Phonetic Society of Japan (音声研究), Vol.24, pp.36-48.
https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/onseikenkyu/24/0/24_36/_article/-char/ja/
© 2020 Phonetic Society of Japan.

A teacher-facing survey, not a learner study, but practically useful: it asks working Japanese
teachers what they find hardest about teaching pitch accent, and concludes that teacher training
should emphasise (a) the ability to judge the pitch of one's own voice and (b) understanding the
gap between "model" pronunciation and what teachers actually produce. Relevant to anyone using
this guide to teach, or self-correcting from a recording, since the paper is explicitly about the
self-assessment-of-one's-own-pitch skill that the guide's "why recording yourself is
non-negotiable" section relies on. In clean English-abstract PDF, extracts and renders fine.

Kohno Morio (1998), "Moora, onsetsu, rizumu no shinrigengogakuteki kousatsu" [モーラ, 音節,
リズムの心理言語学的考察 - "Mora, Syllable, and Rhythm: A Psycholinguistic Study"], Journal of
the Phonetic Society of Japan, Vol.2 No.1, pp.16-24 (April 1998).
https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/onseikenkyu/2/1/2_KJ00007630824/_pdf
© 1998 Phonetic Society of Japan.

The scholarship behind "mora timing", which the guide calls "the thing that decides whether you
are understood" but does not cite. Argues that humans have two separate, neuropsychologically
distinct rhythm-processing systems - a fast "holistic" one (under ~330ms inter-beat intervals)
and a slower "analytic" one (over ~420ms) - and situates Japanese mora timing within that
framework rather than treating "one mora = one beat" as a bare classroom rule. A 1998 scan (9
pages at 400ppi, hence its unusually large file size for a 9-page paper - it is page images, not
reflowed text). English summary extracts cleanly; the Japanese body text has the same
font-encoding quirk as the Matsuzaki paper above.

Kori Shiro (2013), "Hantei youkyuu no shitsumonbun ni okeru gimonkei joushouchou to sono onseiteki
tokuchou" [判定要求の質問文における疑問型上昇調とその音声的特徴 - "Interrogative Rise in Tokyo
Japanese"], Studies in Language and Culture (言語文化研究), Osaka University Graduate School of
Language and Culture, Vol.39, starting p.221.
http://www.lang.osaka-u.ac.jp/~caris/articles/ (author's own departmental page; PDF filename is
the Japanese title)
No separate licence statement found; self-archived by the author on his university page, credit
Kori Shiro / Osaka University.

The guide's Pitch accent chapter is entirely about word-level pitch; this is the closest thing
found here to a scholarly treatment of the guide's other named gap, sentence-level intonation -
specifically, how a plain yes/no question (no か) is produced and perceived in Tokyo Japanese:
final-mora lengthening (40-100ms depending on the word's own accent), a widened pitch range and
faster tempo before the rise, and four sub-types of question (high-necessity, doubtful, positive-
assumption, negative-assumption) that differ acoustically. Written in English (the paper's own
abstract/summary section), citable, 24 pages.
