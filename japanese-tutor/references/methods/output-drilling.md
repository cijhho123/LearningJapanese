# Method: Output / production drilling

**Premise:** producing language forces you to notice the gap between what you can
say and what you mean. That noticing is what directs attention in later input,
and it is the best-supported route out of the intermediate plateau.

## Why this exists as its own profile

Almost every self-study setup - and the entire JLPT - tests **recognition only**.
A learner can clear N2 and be unable to hold a five-minute conversation, and
nothing in their study loop or score report will flag it.

This profile exists so production is a scheduled, non-negotiable thing rather
than something that gets started three years late, in an interview.

## Teaching stance

**They produce first, always.** There is no version of this method where you supply the sentence. The attempt *is* the exercise.

## What this biases toward

- `translate-to-japanese`, `grammar-produce-with-target`, `guided-composition`,
  `roleplay`, `explain-back`
- `vocab-recall` over `vocab-recognition`
- Prompts chosen to force constructions from the learner's current error profile

## Sequencing rule

Pick the target construction from `kb.py weak` - the top recurring error, or the
direction where recall stability lags recognition furthest behind. Production
practice on things they already produce well is wasted.

## How to correct

- Correct **one thing per attempt** - the most important one. Then have them
  redo it. A fully marked-up sentence teaches nothing.
- Say "no" before anything else when it's wrong. Don't bury it.
- Log every failure with the exact wording they used.
- Unnatural-but-grammatical counts as an error worth mentioning, flagged as
  such. 私は on every sentence is the classic.

## What it deliberately does not do

- Does not build vocabulary breadth
- Does not replace input volume - you cannot produce what you have never met

## Failure mode

Production without enough input becomes fluent wrong Japanese, fossilised early.
If they are producing confidently from a 400-word vocabulary, the bottleneck is
input, and more drilling will entrench errors rather than fix them.
