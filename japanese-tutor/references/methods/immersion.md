# Method: Immersion / sentence mining

**Lineage:** AJATT -> MIA -> Refold, plus Tatsumoto's revival.

**Premise:** language is acquired from massive contact with real native
material. SRS is a *scaffold* that makes native material comprehensible sooner -
never the main event. Comprehension precedes production.

## Teaching stance

**Ask lightly.** Mining and hours of contact matter more than being quizzed on each item. Check comprehension, then move - do not turn every mined word into a Socratic exchange.

## What this biases toward

- `sentence-mining-parse`, `vocab-cloze`, `graded-passage` from real material
- Vocabulary first, kanji readings acquired **through words**, not in isolation
- Frequency ordering, weighted toward the learner's actual domains
- Grammar: skim a guide once for the shape, then learn it from exposure

## Sequencing rule

Next item = highest-frequency unknown **in material they are actually consuming**.
Their own mined corpus outranks any general frequency list once mining starts.

## What it deliberately does not do

- No isolated kanji course (no RTK-style keyword drilling)
- No textbook chapter order, no JLPT-driven sequencing
- No grammar study as a standalone subject past the first pass

## Session shape *(a suggestion, never a mandate)*

Warm up on overdue items -> mine 1-3 sentences from something they're reading or
watching -> parse them together -> add what's worth adding.

## Honest failure modes - watch for these and say something

- **Anki becomes the hobby.** Review load creeps up, immersion gets squeezed out,
  and the method inverts itself. If reviews are eating the whole session, cut
  new cards - don't extend the session.
- **Mining bias.** They will mine nouns and content words and never card a
  particle, a collocation, or a register distinction. Compensate deliberately.
- **The 1000-word wall.** The gap between a starter deck and comprehensible
  native content is brutal and this method does not solve it - it tells you to
  endure it. Generated `graded-passage` material is the bridge; use it.
- **Output is never trained.** Nothing here produces speaking ability. If the
  learner's goal involves talking to people, schedule production separately.
